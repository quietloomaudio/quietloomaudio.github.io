LISTENING GUIDE

Brown noise vs pink noise vs white noise

The labels describe different frequency balances—not a ranking of which sound is best.

The short version

White, pink and brown noise are all broad noise textures, but their energy is distributed differently across frequencies. White noise has equal energy per frequency and is often heard as the brightest or most hiss-like option. Pink noise gives relatively more weight to lower frequencies, so it can sound softer and more balanced. Brown noise leans lower again, producing a deeper, warmer character. These are broad descriptions; your headphones, speakers and hearing all change the experience.

When to try each one

Try white or fan-like noise when sharper, higher-frequency distractions such as nearby voices or street activity stand out most. Try pink noise when you want a middle ground that is less bright but still broadly textured. Try brown noise when a low, weighty layer feels more comfortable, especially in a bedroom or when a brighter sound becomes tiring. None of these sounds guarantees sleep or focus. The useful measure is whether it makes the environment easier to inhabit without becoming the main thing you notice.

A simple way to choose

Use each sound at the same low volume for five or ten minutes while doing the intended activity. Avoid rapidly switching back and forth; your ears need a moment to adapt. Keep the one that makes interruptions feel less sharp. If all of them feel intrusive, try a natural environment such as rain or cabin ambience instead. The best background sound is personal, situational and comfortable enough to leave alone.

Brown

Lower and warmer; try it if bright hiss feels fatiguing.

Pink
